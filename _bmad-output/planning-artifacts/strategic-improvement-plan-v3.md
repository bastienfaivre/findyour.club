## 🎯 STRATEGIC BRIEF

findyour.club is a free, multilingual directory where every local club — sports, cultural, social — gets a clean, verified profile page with schedule, photos, how to join, and contact info. It solves two problems simultaneously: visitors waste hours navigating dead websites, municipal PDFs, and Facebook noise trying to find clubs near them, while small clubs remain invisible online because they lack the resources or motivation to maintain a web presence. The platform is built and maintained by a solo volunteer developer in Lausanne, funded by donations, launching first in French-speaking Switzerland. The domain *is* the brand — every URL shared says "find your club" — and the product is remarkably complete for a pre-launch volunteer project: 4 languages, rich club profiles with social links, dynamic OG images, printable promotional materials, structured data for SEO, and a polished club admin experience. The critical challenge now is not product — it's getting the first 50 clubs listed to break the empty-directory problem.

## 5. ADOPTION PATH
*(Framework: The Tipping Point)*

**Bottleneck diagnosis (v3)**: The bottleneck has shifted from **stickiness** (v2: no name, no identity) to **reach** (v3: the product is ready but nobody knows it exists). The platform now has a name, an identity, a professional look, and promotional tools. What it lacks is the first critical mass of clubs.

**The empty directory problem**: A directory with zero clubs is worthless to visitors. A directory with 50+ clubs in one region becomes genuinely useful. The goal is to cross from 0 to 50 as fast as possible. This requires **direct outreach**, not organic growth.

**Connector play (v3 — specific to Romandie)**:
1. **Municipal sports offices** (services des sports) in Lausanne, Geneva, Fribourg, Neuchâtel, Sion. These offices maintain (usually outdated) club lists. Offer to digitize their list — they get a modern, maintained resource; you get 20-50 clubs per city.
2. **Cantonal sports associations** (associations cantonales de sport) — umbrella organizations that federate clubs by canton. One partnership = dozens of clubs.
3. **Expat Facebook group admins** in Swiss cities — they answer "how do I meet people?" weekly. Give them findyour.club as their standard answer.
4. **Relocation companies and HR departments** at EPFL, CERN, UN Geneva, international schools.

**Maven play (v3)**:
1. **Digitally-active club presidents** — find 3-5 clubs that already have a good Facebook/Instagram presence. They're the most likely to understand the value and become ambassadors.
2. **Local community bloggers** in Lausanne/Geneva who write about local life, integration, activities.

**Salesman pitch (v3 — updated with platform name, in French)**:
> "Salut, je suis [prénom], j'ai créé findyour.club. C'est un annuaire gratuit où chaque club a une page complète — horaires, photos, comment s'inscrire. Ton club pourrait avoir sa page en 10 minutes, totalement gratuit, pour toujours. Ça aide les gens à vous trouver, surtout les nouveaux arrivants. Tu veux que je te montre?"

**Stickiness fix (v3)**: No longer the name — that's solved. The new stickiness fix is **the club page preview on the Apply page**. A club president hearing the pitch needs to *see* what their page would look like before investing 10 minutes. Show a real example or interactive mockup. "Here's what your club page will look like on findyour.club" — this makes the abstract concrete and dramatically increases conversion from pitch to application.

**Broken windows (v3)**:
1. **Empty directory** — the #1 broken window. A visitor arriving at findyour.club and seeing zero clubs will never return. **Do not publicly launch until at least 20-30 clubs are listed.** Soft-launch: get clubs listed first, then announce publicly.
2. **No founder video** — the About page text works, but a platform asking for trust without a human face still feels incomplete. Record the video before the public launch announcement.
3. **Limited activity taxonomy** — if a dance club or music group visits and doesn't find their category, they'll assume the platform isn't for them. Expand to 20+ categories before launch.

## M0. POSITIONING FOUNDATION
*(Framework: Obviously Awesome)*

Pulling from Phase 1 and applying the 5-component positioning audit:

- **Competitive alternative**: What clubs do without findyour.club:
  - Have no web presence at all (most small clubs)
  - Maintain a poorly-updated WordPress/Wix site they resent
  - Rely on Facebook page that's hard to find via search
  - Appear on a municipal PDF list that's 3 years outdated
  - Visitors: Google + hope, ask friends, browse Facebook noise

- **Unique attributes**:
  1. Standardized, beautiful profile pages (no design skills needed)
  2. Completely free, forever (not freemium — actually free)
  3. Multilingual (FR/DE/IT/EN) with proper SEO per language
  4. Verified by a human (quality signal)
  5. Promotional materials auto-generated (poster, QR card, badge)
  6. The URL findyour.club is self-explanatory — every link is marketing

- **Value enabled**:
  1. Clubs become findable to anyone searching → more members
  2. Zero cost, zero maintenance burden → no reason not to list
  3. Professional online presence without any technical work → credibility
  4. Physical promotional materials → offline-to-online bridge
  5. Standardized format → visitors can compare and evaluate quickly

- **Best-fit customer (right now)**:
  - **For clubs**: Small-to-medium clubs in French-speaking Switzerland that either (a) have no website or (b) have a neglected Facebook page. They're run by volunteers who don't have time or skills for web maintenance. They lose potential members because people can't find them online.
  - **For visitors**: Newcomers to a city (expats, students, movers) actively looking for activities and social connections. They're Googling "club de [sport] [ville]" and finding nothing useful.

- **Market category**: **Free community directory** — not a "platform" (too vague), not a "search engine" (too technical), not a "social network" (wrong expectations). A directory. The word "directory" is functional and honest. "Free" and "community" modify it.

**Positioning gap diagnosis**: The positioning is now solid. The only gap: the market category "directory" might feel old-fashioned. But findyour.club's design and UX signal modernity, which counteracts the word's connotations. Don't overthink the category name — just be consistent in using "free club directory" in all outreach.

## M1. AUDIENCE AWARENESS & COPY STRATEGY
*(Framework: Breakthrough Advertising)*

### For Club Admins (the supply side — the critical audience at launch)

**Awareness level**: **Level 2 — Problem Aware**

Club presidents know their club is hard to find online. They get comments like "I almost didn't find you" or "I didn't know you existed." They feel the problem but don't know a free, maintained solution exists. They may have tried building a website and given up, or they assume any online presence costs money or requires technical skills.

**Evidence**: The v2 pitch "everyone has this problem — people just can't find us" came from an actual club interaction. The problem is felt and named by the clubs themselves.

**Market sophistication stage**: **Stage 1** — New market. There is no competing claim in the "free club directory for Switzerland" space. Existing alternatives (sport.ch, vereinsverzeichnis.ch) are not actively marketing, have poor UX, and don't position as free. findyour.club can make the bold, simple claim.

**Copy entry point**: Lead with the problem, then introduce the solution.
> "People near you are searching for a club like yours — but they can't find you online."
> Then: "findyour.club gives your club a free page in 10 minutes."

**What NOT to say**:
- Don't lead with the product ("We built a platform that...") — they don't know they need a platform
- Don't lead with features ("Multilingual, SEO-optimized, with JSON-LD...") — irrelevant to club presidents
- Don't compare to competitors they don't know ("Unlike vereinsverzeichnis.ch...") — they've never heard of it
- Don't use tech language ("directory," "listing," "profile page" are fine; "SEO," "OG images," "structured data" are not)

### For Visitors (the demand side — secondary at launch)

**Awareness level**: **Level 1-2 — Unaware to Problem Aware**

Most visitors don't know findyour.club exists. Some are actively searching (Problem Aware — "how do I find a yoga class near me?") and will arrive via Google. Others are passively open (Unaware — they'd join a club if they knew about one, but aren't actively searching).

**Market sophistication stage**: **Stage 1** — Nobody else is claiming this space in their feed.

**Copy entry point**: For organic search arrivals, the club page IS the copy — it needs to be clear, fast, complete. For social/viral arrivals (shared links), the OG card is the copy — it needs to look trustworthy and interesting.

**What NOT to say**: Don't explain the platform to visitors. They don't care about findyour.club — they care about the club they're looking at. The platform should be invisible. Let club pages do the talking.

## M4. NARRATIVE & COPY STRUCTURE
*(Framework: Building a StoryBrand — SB7)*

### SB7 Map for Club Admins (primary audience at launch)

- **Hero**: A volunteer club president who runs a great club but struggles to attract new members
- **External problem**: Their club has no online presence, or their website is outdated and hard to maintain
- **Internal problem**: They feel frustrated that great activities go unnoticed. They've tried Facebook, maybe a website — nothing sticks. They feel like it shouldn't be this hard.
- **Philosophical problem**: Clubs that bring people together shouldn't be invisible. The internet should make finding community *easier*, not harder.
- **Guide (empathy)**: "I moved to a new city and couldn't find a club. When I finally found one, they said 'everyone has this problem.' I get it."
- **Guide (authority)**: Built by a developer, manually verified, already used by [X] clubs in the area. Professional design. Free forever — backed by transparent donation model.
- **Plan**:
  1. Fill in the form (10 minutes)
  2. We review and publish your page
  3. People find your club
- **Direct CTA**: "List your club — free" / "Get started — it takes 10 minutes"
- **Transitional CTA**: "See an example page" (link to a real club page)
- **Stakes**: Your club stays invisible. Potential members never find you. They join a different club — or worse, they stay home.
- **Success vision**: New members showing up saying "I found you on findyour.club." Your club growing. Your notice board has a QR code linking to your page. Parents searching for activities for their kids find you in seconds.

### SB7 Map for Visitors (secondary — shorter)

- **Hero**: Someone looking for an activity, club, or community near them
- **Problem**: Can't find clear, reliable info. Scattered across Google, Facebook, PDFs.
- **Guide**: findyour.club — every club, same format, verified
- **Plan**: Search → Find → Show up
- **CTA**: Search clubs / Pick your country
- **Stakes**: You keep searching and give up. Or you stay home.
- **Success**: You found your club. You show up Thursday.

## M5. RETENTION STRATEGY
*(Framework: Hooked)*

**Status**: *(Partially deferred — pre-launch. Design the retention loop now, implement after first 30 clubs.)*

### For Club Admins (the supply side — retention = keeping pages updated)

- **Internal trigger to own**: The feeling "I should update our club info" — triggered by season changes (new schedule), events, new photos, or seeing other clubs' pages.

- **External trigger plan**:
  - **Monthly email**: "Your club page was viewed [X] times this month. Here's a tip: clubs with 3+ photos get [Y]% more views." — delivers value (stats), prompts action (add photos), variable reward (different stats each month).
  - **Seasonal prompt** (September, January): "New season? Update your schedule on findyour.club." — timed to when clubs actually change their programs.
  - **Post-message notification**: When a visitor messages the club through the platform, email the club admin. This is currently missing and is a **critical gap** — messages without notifications are dead messages.

- **Variable reward**: Page view stats (hunt), new member inquiries (tribe), profile completeness score (self).

- **Investment mechanic**: Every photo uploaded, every schedule entry added, every description written increases the value of the page and the switching cost. The more complete the page, the less likely the club is to abandon it.

- **Re-engagement sequence**: If a club hasn't logged in for 60 days → email: "Your club page on findyour.club was viewed [X] times since your last visit. Everything still up to date? [Update your page →]"

### For Visitors (the demand side — retention = returning to discover more)

Visitors don't need to "retain" in the traditional sense — they arrive with a need (find a club), fulfill it, and leave. The platform's job is to be the *first answer* next time they or someone they know needs a club.

- **Internal trigger to own**: "I want to try something new" or "someone just moved here"
- **External trigger**: The shared link (WhatsApp, social) or the printed poster in a gym/community center
- **Retention is not the goal for visitors** — conversion (visitor → club member) is. The platform succeeds when the visitor *leaves* findyour.club and *shows up* at a club.

### 3. Channel experiment brief (Direct Outreach — the Bullseye pick)
